### 1. What are my fundamental rights?
_legal → legal_

Your fundamental rights under the Constitution of India are the core protections the State owes to you. Here is what the Constitution gives you:

- **Equality (Art. 14)** – The State cannot deny you equality before the law or equal protection of the laws. This applies to every person, not just citizens.
- **No Discrimination (Art. 15)** – The State cannot discriminate against a citizen on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth. It also bars denying access to shops, restaurants, hotels, public places on these grounds.
- **Freedom of Religion (Art. 25)** – You have freedom of conscience and the right to profess, practise and propagate religion, subject to public order, morality, health and other fundamental rights.
- **Freedoms (Art. 19)** – You have the right to freedom of speech and expression; to assemble peacefully without arms; to form associations or unions; to move freely throughout India; to reside and settle anywhere; and to practise any profession or carry on any trade or business. The State can impose reasonable restrictions on these.
- **Life and Personal Liberty (Art. 21)** – No person can be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law. Applies to every person, not only citizens.
- **Right to Education (Art. 21A)** – The State must provide free and compulsory education to children aged 6 to 14.
- **Protection against Exploitation (Art. 23)** – Trafficking in human beings, begar (forced unpaid work) and similar forced labour are prohibited.
- **Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art. 32)** – You have the right to approach the Supreme Court to enforce your fundamental rights. The Court can issue writs like habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto and certiorari.

These rights are mainly enforceable against the State (Central and State Governments, Parliament, State Legislatures, and local or other authorities under Government control).

### 3. How can I file an FIR?
_legal → legal_

You can file an FIR (First Information Report) by following these steps:

1. **Go to the nearest police station** where the offence occurred (or any police station for a cognizable offence). Bring any evidence you have – documents, photos, videos, receipts, or witness details.
2. **State the facts clearly**: tell the officer what happened, when, where, who was involved, and the nature of the offence. It helps to write a short note beforehand so you don’t miss anything.
3. **Ask for a written copy** of the FIR. The police must read it back to you and you can ask for corrections before they sign it. Insist on getting the FIR number and a copy (usually for a nominal fee).
4. **If the police refuse** to register a cognizable offence, you can:
   - Write a complaint to the Superintendent of Police (SP) of the district, or
   - Send a written request (via email or post) to the Commissioner of Police, or
   - Approach the local magistrate under Section 154 of the BNS (which continues the old CrPC provision) for a direction to register the FIR.
5. **Keep all records**: the FIR copy, the receipt of any fee paid, and any correspondence with the police. These will be useful if you need to approach higher authorities later.

If you need a draft of a written complaint to the SP or help practising the conversation with the police, just let me know.

6. **Other licences/approvals** – Depending on the sector (e.g., food, pharma, education), you may need specific licences from the relevant authority.
7. **Open a business bank account** – Use the incorporation certificate, PAN, and other KYC documents.
8. **Maintain statutory compliance** – Keep books of accounts, file annual returns, conduct board meetings, and comply with labour laws if you have employees.
